# V-GAP — Why the firmware ACF (40.6%) underperforms the host sweep (53.1%)

**[HOST-ONLY — no firmware edit, no flash, no commit.]** Adversarial validation of
claim **C3a**. Default posture: NOT VERIFIED until measured. Replica + ablation:
`scripts/regression-harness/acf_gap_ablation.py`; window-isolation probe:
`/tmp/vgap_probe2.py` (transient, reproduced inline below).

## Claim under attack (C3a)

> "The firmware ACF (in-range Acc2 40.6%) underperforms the clean host sweep
> (53.1%) BECAUSE of three firmware-specific factors absent from the sweep: the
> live log-Gaussian prior, the decayed 512-sample window (SB_NOVELTY_DECAY=0.999/
> emit), and the winner hysteresis."

**Verdict: REFUTED for {prior, hysteresis}; PARTIALLY-CONFIRMED-then-superseded for
the window.** The decay is a minor contributor (+3.1pp). The dominant cause is the
*finite rolling 512-sample window itself*, not its 0.999 decay and not the other two
named factors.

## Anchors (both independently measured here)

| Quantity | Source | Acc1 (in-rng) | Acc2 (in-rng) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Firmware (UNMODIFIED sb_tempo.cpp, host-compiled) | `tempo_accuracy.py` run, 32 in-range tracks | 28.1% | **40.6%** |
| Host sweep `44.44Hz / int / raw` | `acf_ceiling_sweep.py` table | 12.5% | 46.9% |
| Host sweep `44.44Hz / parab / raw` | `acf_ceiling_sweep.py` table | 12.5% | **53.1%** |
| Host sweep `133.33Hz / int / raw` | `acf_ceiling_sweep.py` table | 21.9% | 53.1% |

The 40.6% firmware anchor was confirmed by re-running `tempo_accuracy.py` (which
compiles the real `sb_tempo.cpp`): `IN-RANGE Acc1=28.1% Acc2=40.6%`, octaves
`{off:18, x1:13, x2:5}`, 13/32 = 40.6%. The 53.1% sweep figure is the
firmware-rate (44.44 Hz) parabolic ACF ceiling — the correct comparator, since the
firmware also runs at 44.44 Hz with parabolic interpolation.

## Step 1 — Firmware-faithful replica REPRODUCES 40.6% (=> not a code bug)

The replica (`acf_gap_ablation.py`) reproduces sb_tempo's winner pipeline line-for-line:
peak-hold /3 decimation (cpp:506-544); a 512 ring decayed by 0.999 every emit with the
new sample written undecayed (cpp:542-544); the per-emit EMA autoranger (cpp:157-165);
per-emit ACF salience over the linearised ring (mean-subtract, biased ACF over the
firmware lag band, per-bin value at each bin's real lag with 3-point parabolic interp,
normalise to max=1; cpp:230-279); `score = salience * log-Gaussian tactus prior`
(cpp:285-292); 96-bin integer argmax with +10%/5-tick hysteresis (cpp:295-333); settled
detection = mode of integer winner BPM over the last 50% of emits (`tempo_accuracy.detected_bpm`).

> **base (full faithful replica): Acc1 = 28.1%, Acc2 = 40.6%** — point-identical to the
> compiled-firmware anchor. The firmware behaviour is FULLY explained by the modelled
> factors; **V-ACFCODE is NOT triggered** (no unexplained firmware-vs-replica divergence).

## Step 2 — Single-factor ablation (from the faithful replica)

| Config (one factor removed) | n | Acc1 | Acc2 | Δ Acc2 vs base |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **base** (full firmware-faithful) | 32 | 28.1% | **40.6%** | — |
| −prior (drop log-Gaussian tactus prior) | 32 | 25.0% | 40.6% | **+0.0** |
| −decay (undecayed ring, decay=1.0) | 32 | 31.2% | 43.8% | **+3.1** |
| −hyst (instantaneous argmax, no hysteresis) | 32 | 28.1% | 40.6% | **+0.0** |
| argmax(method) — sweep-style global ACF argmax | 32 | 18.8% | 34.4% | **−6.2** |
| −bins(cont) — de-quantise the 96-bin grid | 32 | 28.1% | 40.6% | **+0.0** |
| +133hz(rate) — faithful pipeline at full 133.3 Hz | 32 | 25.0% | 37.5% | **−3.1** |
| all-off (−prior & −decay & −hyst) | 32 | 18.8% | 31.2% | **−9.4** |

### What this kills

- **Prior: REFUTED.** Removing the log-Gaussian tactus prior moves Acc2 by **0.0 pp**.
  It cannot be a cause of the gap.
- **Hysteresis: REFUTED.** Removing hysteresis (winner = instantaneous argmax every
  emit) moves Acc2 by **0.0 pp**. The note in the claim that "hysteresis converges over
  a track" is correct — and it is therefore irrelevant to the settled-window score.
- **Decay: minor.** Undecaying the ring buys only **+3.1 pp** (40.6 → 43.8), i.e. ~¼ of
  the ~12.5 pp gap to 53.1%. Not the dominant cause.
- **Adversarial method probe BACKFIRES on the claim's framing:** switching to the
  sweep's *global-argmax-over-ACF* method (the structural difference of the 53.1% sweep)
  inside the firmware's rolling ring makes it **worse** (−6.2 pp), not better. So the
  per-bin-vs-argmax method is NOT the recovery lever either.
- **Bin quantisation: neutral.** Continuous-BPM (parabolic over the salience grid)
  de-quantising the 96 integer bins moves Acc2 by **0.0 pp**.
- **Rate: minor negative.** The faithful per-bin pipeline at full 133 Hz is **worse**
  (−3.1 pp), because at 133 Hz the per-bin lags are coarser-relative and the rolling-ring
  damage dominates — the rate is not the lever the claim omitted in the helpful direction.

The `all-off` row (−9.4 pp) confirms the three named factors do not *additively* explain
the gap — removing all three makes it WORSE, because the prior/hysteresis are mildly
stabilising, not harmful.

## The single dominant cause — the rolling 512-sample WINDOW

A targeted probe holds the sweep's estimator (global ACF argmax + parabolic, no prior,
no bin quantisation, peak-hold /3 to 44.44 Hz) FIXED and varies ONLY the analysis window:

| Probe (44.44 Hz, global-argmax, no prior, no quant) | n | Acc1 | Acc2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `wholeclip_44_int` (whole-clip ACF, integer) | 32 | 12.5% | 46.9% |
| `wholeclip_44_parab` (whole-clip ACF, parabolic) | 32 | 12.5% | **53.1%** |
| `rollring_44_argmax` (firmware rolling decayed-512 ring, parabolic) | 32 | 15.6% | **37.5%** |
| `rollring_44_argmax_nodecay` (rolling-512 ring, decay=1.0) | 32 | 15.6% | 34.4% |

- `wholeclip_44_int` = **46.9%** and `wholeclip_44_parab` = **53.1%** reproduce the
  published sweep rows `44.44Hz/int/raw` (46.9%) and `44.44Hz/parab/raw` (53.1%)
  **exactly** — the probe harness is faithful to `acf_ceiling_sweep.py`.
- Swapping ONLY the window — sweep's whole-clip ACF → firmware's rolling decayed-512 ring,
  everything else held identical — drops Acc2 from **53.1% → 37.5%**, a **−15.6 pp** fall
  that *exceeds and subsumes* the whole 40.6→53.1 gap.
- Decay vs no-decay on the rolling ring: 37.5% vs 34.4% — so the **0.999 decay is mildly
  HELPFUL on the rolling window**, and the damage is the **finite 512-sample window
  itself** (~11.5 s of novelty vs the whole ~25 s clip), not its decay.

The firmware integrates its ACF over a rolling ~512-sample (~11.5 s) window that is
re-decayed every emit; the sweep autocorrelates the **entire clip** once. On these
~25 s HarmonixSet clips the whole-clip ACF has ~2× the periodicity evidence and a much
cleaner dominant lag, which is the bulk of the 12.5 pp gap.

## Reconciling the apparent paradox (method probe vs sweep)

The sweep's `argmax` method scores 53.1% but `argmax(method)` in the ablation scores
34.4%. There is no contradiction: the sweep's argmax runs over the **whole undecayed
clip**, whereas the ablation's `argmax(method)` runs the same argmax over the **rolling
decayed-512 ring**. The method is not the variable that matters — the *window the method
operates on* is. Holding method fixed and toggling only the window (the probe table above)
is the clean isolation, and it lands the entire gap on the window.

## Tunable or bug?

**Tunable / structural, not a code bug.** The replica reproduced the firmware's 40.6%
to the point, so there is no firmware-vs-spec divergence to chase. The dominant lever
(`SB_HISTORY_LENGTH` = 512 samples at 44.44 Hz, plus the per-emit rolling re-ACF) is a
design parameter. Lengthening the analysis window toward whole-clip-equivalent
periodicity evidence (longer ring, or an accumulating rather than rolling decayed ACF)
is the change that would move the firmware toward the 53.1% ceiling; tuning the prior,
removing hysteresis, or de-quantising the bins would not.

## Caveats / scope

- 32 in-range HarmonixSet tracks (60–155 BPM); ±1 track = ±3.1 pp, so the +3.1 pp decay
  delta is a single-track effect and within noise — do not over-read it. The 15.6 pp
  window effect is 5 tracks and well outside that noise.
- Host clean novelty (`novelty_from_wav`). On-device sb_tempo consumes the AGC-clamped
  GDFT novelty, which is *worse*, so 40.6% is itself an optimistic firmware bound; the
  window finding (a relative, same-novelty comparison) is unaffected by that absolute caveat.
- Hysteresis was simulated per-emit, not assumed converged; it still measured +0.0 pp,
  so the "ignore hysteresis" simplification is validated rather than assumed.
