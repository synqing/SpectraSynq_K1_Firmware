### Why the ACF is flat

The host novelty for these tracks is near-DC (e.g. Bottoms Up: mean=12480, non-zero
3328/3329 frames). The full autocorrelation is therefore a monotone decay:
ACF[lag] decreases linearly with lag. The normalised window from lag 38 (210 BPM) to
lag 145 (55 BPM) has std=0.02878 for every track in the corpus — there is no
tempo-periodic structure to pick. The highest salience always falls at the shortest
lag (210 BPM top-of-window), not at any harmonic of the GT.

The reason sal@2×GT (0.983–0.992) exceeds sal@GT (0.930–0.948) is arithmetic:
2×GT lies at a shorter lag than GT, and shorter lags have higher values in a
monotone-decay ACF. This is not "ACF peaks at the harmonic"; it is the short-lag
bias inherent to an unconverged or non-periodic novelty signal.

### True root cause of x2 errors

All 5 x2 error tracks have GT BPM in the 060–085 range. The Goertzel spectrum
integrates energy at each candidate BPM frequency over time. For these tracks
(R&B/pop with strong off-beat or syncopated sub-divisions), the Goertzel bins at
~140–155 BPM accumulate more energy than the bins at ~70–85 BPM. The quartic
exaggeration in sb_update_tempo then amplifies that already-taller bin to dominate.
This is the same **low-bin-pinning / quartic-exaggeration** mechanism that drives the
"off" errors, applied specifically to the 060–085 BPM victim bucket — not ACF
harmonic-multiple ambiguity.

The prior penalises 71–85 BPM more heavily than 140–155 BPM because 120 BPM is

## Evidence — C3c

### Arbiter simulation — threshold scan on full in-range corpus (n=32)

**Baseline:** Acc1=9/32=28.1%, Acc2=13/32=40.6%, octave-err=12.5%

The arbiter rule tested: if ACF_salience(det/2) ≥ THRESH × ACF_salience(det),
switch winner to det/2; else if ACF_salience(det×2) ≥ THRESH × ACF_salience(det),
switch to det×2; else keep det.

**No threshold produces a net Acc1 gain. Acc2 is unchanged at every threshold.**
The best threshold (0.95) trades 3 regressions for 2 fixes, net Acc1 −1 track (−3.1pp).

### Why the arbiter fails

Because the ACF is flat (std=0.029), the ratio sal(det/2)/sal(det) is in the range
0.929–0.952 for x2 error tracks — but the same ratio is 0.932–0.940 for most
**correctly-detected** tracks (where det is already right). The ACF salience provides
no discrimination between "winner is correct" and "winner is an octave error": the
flat monotone-decay makes both look identical. Any threshold permissive enough to
halve the x2 errors is also permissive enough to break tracks that are correct.

## Summary

- **Baseline (committed detector):** in-range Acc1=28.1%, Acc2=40.6%, octave-err=12.5%;
  octave histogram x1:12, x2:5, off:15.
- **x2 per-track:** all 5 have GT in 060–085 BPM and det in 140–155 BPM. The ratio
  det/GT is 1.824–2.027, consistent with x2 label, but the ACF does NOT peak at
  2×GT on any track. ACF peak is at 210.5 BPM (top of window) for all 5.
- **C3b REFUTED.** ACF harmonic-multiple ambiguity is not the mechanism. The cause is
  monotone-decay short-lag bias combined with quartic-amplified Goertzel energy at
  high-BPM bins for the 060–085 BPM victim bucket.
- **C3c REFUTED.** The half/double arbiter cannot discriminate correct from octave-
  erroneous winners using flat-ACF salience ratios. At best threshold (0.95) it
  regresses 3 correct tracks and fixes 2 x2 errors, net −1. It does not reduce
  octave-err without hurting Acc1.
- **What does work:** fixing the quartic low-bin-pinning (quartic reduction / bin
  spectrum shape restoration) is the load-bearing lever identified in the existing
  plan. The log-Gaussian prior and ACF salience are correct architecturally but
  cannot compensate for a flattened ACF or a quartic-dominated spectrum alone.
